**Scope:** Formal mathematical conventions, data schemas, loss-sign rules, distributional models, and validation invariants for probabilistic portfolio risk analysis.

## 1. Core Principles & Semantic Distinctions

### 1.1 Non-Prediction & Research Disclaimer
Monte Carlo simulation outputs, Value at Risk (VaR), and Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) are **probabilistic risk assessments conditional on specified model assumptions, historical estimates, and distributional parameters**.
- They do **not** constitute price predictions, guaranteed profit forecasts, or proof that real-world portfolio losses will never exceed simulated thresholds.
- Real-world financial asset returns frequently exhibit regime shifts, liquidity freezes, non-stationary volatility, and structural breaks that are not fully captured by stationary distributional models.
- All API responses and dashboard visualizations must explicitly convey these limitations.

### 1.2 Separation of Estimator Families
The platform strictly separates three distinct risk estimator families and never combines them under one ambiguous label:

1. **Monte Carlo Risk Estimator:**
   Calculated from $M$ simulated paths over horizon $T$ generated by a specified joint distribution (e.g., Multivariate Normal or Multivariate Student-t).
2. **Parametric (Variance-Covariance) Risk Estimator:**
   Calculated analytically under the assumption of joint normality over horizon $T$ using estimated periodic mean $\mu_p$ and periodic portfolio volatility $\sigma_p$:
   $$\operatorname{VaR}_{q, \text{param}} = V_0 \cdot \left(-\mu_p T + z_q \sigma_p \sqrt{T}\right)$$
   $$\operatorname{CVaR}_{q, \text{param}} = V_0 \cdot \left(-\mu_p T + \frac{\phi(z_q)}{1 - q} \sigma_p \sqrt{T}\right)$$
   where $z_q = \Phi^{-1}(q)$, $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF, and $\phi$ is the standard normal PDF.
3. **Historical Risk Estimator:**
   Calculated from empirical historical returns observed over rolling windows in the active dataset, with zero distributional assumptions.

## 2. Mathematical Formulations & Portfolio Evolution

### 2.1 Initial Portfolio Valuation & Cash Allocation
Let a portfolio consist of $n$ assets with initial weights $\mathbf{w} = (w_1, \dots, w_n)^T$ and initial capital $V_0 > 0$.
The cash reserve $C$ is:
$$C = V_0 \left(1 - \sum_{i=1}^n w_i\right)$$
where $0 \le \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \le 1.0$ and $w_i \ge 0$ for long-only portfolios.
The initial market value of asset $i$ is:
$$V_{i,0} = w_i \cdot V_0$$

### 2.2 Asset Return Generation & Distribution Models

#### A. Multivariate Normal Model
For each period $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$ and simulation path $m \in \{1, \dots, M\}$:
$$\mathbf{r}_{t}^{(m)} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \mathbf{L} \mathbf{z}_{t}^{(m)}$$
where:
- $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the periodic expected asset return vector.
- $\mathbf{\Sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is the sample covariance matrix.
- $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is the lower triangular Cholesky factor satisfying $\mathbf{L} \mathbf{L}^T = \mathbf{\Sigma}$.
- $\mathbf{z}_{t}^{(m)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_n)$ is a vector of independent standard normal random variables.

If $\mathbf{\Sigma}$ has negative eigenvalues due to numerical precision ($\lambda_{\min} < 0$), the engine performs spectral positive semi-definite (PSD) projection:
$$\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\text{psd}} = \sum_{k=1}^n \max(\lambda_k, 10^{-8}) \mathbf{v}_k \mathbf{v}_k^T$$
before computing $\mathbf{L}$.

#### B. Multivariate Student-t Model (Heavy Tails)
To model fat tails and excess kurtosis, the engine supports a multivariate Student-t distribution with degrees of freedom $\nu > 2$:
$$\mathbf{r}_{t}^{(m)} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \sqrt{\frac{\nu - 2}{\nu}} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{\nu}{W_{t}^{(m)}}} \cdot \mathbf{L} \mathbf{z}_{t}^{(m)}$$
where:
- $W_{t}^{(m)} \sim \chi^2_\nu$ is drawn from a Chi-squared distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom.
- The factor $\sqrt{\frac{\nu - 2}{\nu}}$ scales the scale matrix such that the theoretical covariance of the generated returns exactly matches $\mathbf{\Sigma}$:
  $$\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{r}_t) = \frac{\nu}{\nu - 2} \cdot \left(\frac{\nu - 2}{\nu} \mathbf{\Sigma}\right) = \mathbf{\Sigma}$$
- Degrees of freedom parameter is constrained to $\nu \in [2.5, 30.0]$.

### 2.3 Portfolio Wealth Evolution Over Time

#### A. Buy-and-Hold Compounding (Default)
In a buy-and-hold portfolio, no interim rebalancing occurs. Each asset compounds independently:
$$V_{i,t}^{(m)} = \max\left(0, V_{i,t-1}^{(m)} \cdot (1 + r_{i,t}^{(m)})\right)$$
where asset prices floor at zero (limited liability; $V_{i,t} \ge 0$).
Total portfolio value at time $t$ for path $m$ is:
$$V_t^{(m)} = C + \sum_{i=1}^n V_{i,t}^{(m)}$$
Asset weights drift naturally over time:
$$w_{i,t}^{(m)} = \frac{V_{i,t}^{(m)}}{V_t^{(m)}}$$

#### B. Constant-Weight Periodic Rebalancing
Under constant-weight rebalancing, the portfolio is rebalanced to target weights $\mathbf{w}$ at the end of each period:
$$V_t^{(m)} = V_{t-1}^{(m)} \cdot \left(1 + \sum_{i=1}^n w_i r_{i,t}^{(m)}\right)$$

### 2.4 Terminal Loss Definition & Sign Convention
Let $V_T^{(m)}$ denote the terminal portfolio value at horizon $T$ for path $m$.
1. **Monetary Loss:**
   $$L^{(m)} = V_0 - V_T^{(m)}$$
2. **Fractional (Percentage) Loss:**
   $$L_{\%}^{(m)} = \frac{V_0 - V_T^{(m)}}{V_0} = -\frac{V_T^{(m)} - V_0}{V_0}$$

**Loss-Sign Invariant:**
- A positive loss ($L > 0$) indicates an economic loss ($V_T < V_0$).
- A negative loss ($L < 0$) indicates an economic gain ($V_T > V_0$).
- Value at Risk ($\operatorname{VaR}$) and Conditional Value at Risk ($\operatorname{CVaR}$) are reported as **positive values for positive losses**.
- Example: $\operatorname{VaR}_{95\%} = \$120,000$ indicates that the 95th percentile loss is \$120,000 (i.e., with 95% confidence, the portfolio loss will not exceed \$120,000 over horizon $T$).

## 3. Value at Risk (VaR) and Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR)

### 3.1 Empirical Value at Risk ($\operatorname{VaR}_q$)
For a confidence level $q \in (0, 1)$ (e.g. $q = 0.95$ or $q = 0.99$), empirical $\operatorname{VaR}_q$ is the $q$-quantile of the sorted empirical losses $\{L_{(1)} \le L_{(2)} \le \dots \le L_{(M)}\}$:
$$\operatorname{VaR}_q = Q_q(L)$$
The quantile is evaluated using standard linear interpolation (`method='linear'`):
$$Q_q(L) = L_{\lfloor k \rfloor} + (k - \lfloor k \rfloor) (L_{\lceil k \rceil} - L_{\lfloor k \rfloor})$$
where $k = (M - 1) q + 1$.

### 3.2 Empirical Conditional Value at Risk ($\operatorname{CVaR}_q$)
Also known as Expected Shortfall ($\operatorname{ES}_q$), $\operatorname{CVaR}_q$ measures the average loss incurred given that the loss is at or beyond the $\operatorname{VaR}_q$ threshold:
$$\operatorname{CVaR}_q = \mathbb{E}\left[L \mid L \ge \operatorname{VaR}_q\right]$$

For a finite sample of $M$ simulated losses $\{L^{(1)}, \dots, L^{(M)}\}$:
1. Filter the tail subset $\mathcal{T}_q = \{L^{(m)} : L^{(m)} \ge \operatorname{VaR}_q\}$.
2. If $|\mathcal{T}_q| > 0$:
   $$\operatorname{CVaR}_q = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_q|} \sum_{L \in \mathcal{T}_q} L$$
3. By mathematical definition:
   $$\operatorname{CVaR}_q \ge \operatorname{VaR}_q$$
   Any violation of this inequality indicates numerical degeneration or an invalid estimator implementation.

## 4. Summary Statistics & Diagnostic Metrics

A completed risk simulation must produce:
1. **Initial Wealth:** $V_0$.
2. **Expected Terminal Wealth:** $\bar{V}_T = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M V_T^{(m)}$.
3. **Median Terminal Wealth:** $\operatorname{Median}(V_T)$.
4. **Terminal Quantiles:** 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 95th percentiles of terminal wealth.
5. **Expected Terminal Return:** $\bar{R}_T = \frac{\bar{V}_T - V_0}{V_0}$.
6. **Standard Deviation of Terminal Return:** $\sigma(R_T)$.
7. **Probability of Loss:**
   $$P(\text{Loss}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \mathbb{I}_{\{V_T^{(m)} < V_0\}}$$
8. **Probability of Exceeding Threshold:** $P(L > \text{Threshold})$ for a user-specified loss threshold.
9. **Extreme Values:**
   - $\min(V_T)$: Observed minimum terminal value across paths (explicitly labeled as a sample extremum, not an absolute theoretical lower bound).
   - $\max(V_T)$: Observed maximum terminal value across paths.
10. **VaR and CVaR Measures:** Absolute monetary values and percentage values at requested confidence levels (e.g. 95%, 99%).
11. **Sample Trajectories:** A bounded subset of $K \le 50$ equity paths for visual fan charting, plus median and 5th/95th percentile curves.

## 5. Reproducibility & Resource Constraints

### 5.1 Determinism & Random Seed Policy
- All Monte Carlo simulations are strictly deterministic when supplied with an integer seed:
  $$\text{Seed} \in [0, 2^{32} - 1]$$
- If the caller does not specify a seed, the server generates a cryptographically secure random 32-bit integer, returns it in the response payload, and stores it in the database for reproducible replay.

### 5.2 Computational Limits
To prevent denial-of-service and out-of-memory errors on concurrent researcher sessions:
- **Paths $M$:** Minimum 100 paths; Maximum 10,000 paths; Default 1,000 paths.
- **Horizon $T$:** Minimum 1 day; Maximum 252 trading days (1 year); Default 21 days (1 month).
- **Assets $N$:** Minimum 1; Maximum 50 assets.
- **Degrees of freedom $\nu$ (Student-t):** Minimum 2.5; Maximum 30.0.
- **Confidence levels $q$:** Valid range $q \in [0.50, 0.9999]$.

### 5.3 Currency Invariants
- All constituent assets must share a single uniform currency (e.g. `USD` or `PKR`).
- Cross-currency portfolios are rejected with HTTP 422 until a formal FX stochastic process is integrated.
